Common questions

How is net per hour worked out?

Take the total pay, tip included, and subtract what the miles cost you in fuel, upkeep and wear on the car. Divide what is left by every hour the offer takes: the trip time the app shows, any wait at the restaurant or store, and the unpaid trip back. That is your net per hour, and it is the number to hold up against what your time is worth.

Why do return miles and waiting time count?

Because you spend them either way. If you wait ten minutes for a bag, or drive two miles back toward the busy part of town because nothing is near the drop-off, that time and fuel belong to this offer. Leaving them out is how a thin offer looks like a good one. If your next offer usually starts right where this one ends, leave the trip back at 0.

What is a good rate per mile?

There is no single answer. Many drivers use rules of thumb between $1.50 and $2.00 a mile, but the right number depends on your car, your city and the time of day. A high rate per mile can still be a bad hour if the offer is slow, which is why net per hour is the better measure. Set both goals and the stricter one decides.

Why is my cost per mile more than the price of gas?

Gas is only part of it. Tires, oil, brakes and repairs wear out with every mile, and the car loses value too, so Drive Rate adds fuel, maintenance and tires, depreciation and anything else you list. The IRS standard mileage rate, shown under My car, is a tax figure meant to cover the average cost of driving for business. It is a useful comparison, but it is not your real cost, and it is not where Drive Rate starts.

Do tips count?

Yes. A tip is income, so add it to the pay. Many apps show the tip separately or only after delivery, so use the tip field when you know it. Without it, the numbers are on the cautious side.

How much should I set aside for taxes?

That depends on your whole year, so Drive Rate gives you a planning number and nothing more. As a self-employed driver you generally owe income tax and self-employment tax on what is left after business costs. It starts at 20 percent of net profit, which you can change. Ask a tax professional what fits your situation, and see the IRS page on managing taxes for gig work. This is not tax advice.

Does it connect to my account or use GPS?

No. It does not connect to any delivery or rideshare account, it does not ask for your location, and nothing you type leaves your device. Your numbers, your log and your saved days stay in this browser. Clearing your browser data removes them.

How the math worksTap to open

One offer

Gross pay = pay + tip

Total miles = miles + miles back

Total minutes = minutes + extra wait + minutes back

Hours = total minutes / 60

Car cost = total miles x cost per mile

Net = gross pay - car cost

Net per hour = net / hours

Gross per mile = gross pay / total miles

Net per mile = net / total miles

Set aside = net x tax percent, only when net is above zero

You keep = net - set aside

Cost per mile

Gas: price per gallon / miles per gallon. Electric: price per kWh / miles per kWh.

Cost per mile = fuel + maintenance and tires + depreciation + other. Or the number you typed, if you know it.

The verdict, checked in this order

1. No goals set, when both goals are blank.

2. Pass, when net is zero or less: you would lose money.

3. Take it, when every goal you set is met: net per hour is at least your hourly goal and gross per mile is at least your per mile goal.

4. Borderline, when net is above zero and every goal you set is at least 80 percent met.

5. Pass, otherwise.

Goals are compared on the exact numbers, not the rounded ones shown. An offer with no pay yet is not judged.

The pay that would meet your goals

For the hourly goal: goal x hours + car cost

For the per mile goal: goal x total miles

Pay needed = the larger of the two you set, rounded up to the next cent, and always more than the car cost.

A day

Gross = the sum of your entries' pay

Miles driven = your odometer figure, or the paid miles plus the miles back of every entry

Hours online = the hours you typed, or the minutes of every entry / 60

Car cost = miles driven x cost per mile. Net = gross - car cost

Set aside = net x tax percent when net is above zero. Take home = net - set aside

Mileage value = miles driven x the IRS business rate for that date, an estimate only

The last 7 days add up every saved day dated today or in the 6 days before.
