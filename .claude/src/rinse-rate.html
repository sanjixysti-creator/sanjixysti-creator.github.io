Common questions

How do I work out what a pressure washing job paid per hour?

Take what you charged and subtract everything the job cost you: chemicals, fuel and wear on the truck and machine, helpers' pay, payment fees and your share of overhead. What is left is your profit. Divide it by every hour you spent, including driving and setup, and you have your real hourly pay.

Which costs should I count?

Count anything you would not have spent without this job: chemicals and supplies, the miles you drive, wear on your machine, pay for helpers, card fees, dump fees and rentals. Then add overhead, which is the cost of running the business: insurance, software, ads, phone and accounting.

How do I find my overhead percent?

Add up what the business costs to run in a year, such as insurance, software, advertising, phone, accounting and storage. Divide that by your yearly sales. The result is the share of every job's price that goes to overhead.

Should I count drive time and setup?

Yes. You are on the clock for them even when nobody pays for them separately, and they are easy to forget. Leaving them out makes far away jobs and jobs with a long setup look better than they are.

What pay per hour should I aim for?

That is your call. It depends on your market, your costs and what you need to take home. Pick a number, enter it as your goal, and Rinse Rate works out the price that gets you there. Remember that what you earn here comes before income tax and self employment tax.

Why is the price for my goal higher than my costs plus my pay?

Card fees and overhead take a share of whatever you charge, so each extra dollar of price only keeps part of itself. Rinse Rate allows for that, along with the helper pay and machine wear that grow with the hours.

Does Rinse Rate save my numbers?

Only in your own browser, on this device. Nothing is sent anywhere. Clearing your browser data removes them.

How the math worksTap to open

Everyone on the job is on the clock for the whole time: hours on site, driving and setup. Your profit is the price minus every cost, and your pay per hour is that profit divided by those hours. Helpers are paid by the hour out of the costs, so what is left is yours.

The price for your goal solves the same sum backwards. Fees and overhead are a share of the price, so the price is your goal pay plus the costs that do not depend on the price, divided by what is left of each dollar after fees and overhead.
