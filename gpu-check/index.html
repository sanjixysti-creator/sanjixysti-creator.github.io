          Test it before you pay

          1. Match the card to the listing. Compare the model name on the card and the box, the ports and the power connectors with the maker's spec page. Some fakes are old cards with a new looking cooler, so a VGA port or an SLI connector on a modern model is a red flag.
          2. Run GPU-Z. It shows the GPU name, memory type and size and the release date. Compare them with the real specs for the model. It can flag some fake cards.
          3. Stress it for 15 to 20 minutes with FurMark or a 3DMark test. Watch for crashes, colored blocks or lines on screen and very high temperatures.
          4. Check the video memory with the OCCT video memory test, and watch temperatures and power draw with HWiNFO.
          5. Look it over. Rattling fans, bent fins, a yellowed board near the chip, missing screws or peeled stickers all deserve a question.
          6. Ask about the warranty. Ask for the box, the receipt and the serial number, and ask the maker whether the warranty passes to a new owner. Some do not.

          If you cannot test where you meet, ask for a live video of the card running first, and think hard before you pay without a test.

          Common questions

          Does it scan the marketplaces for me?

          No. It does not pull in listings. It opens searches for your card on the big marketplaces, you pick the listings you like, and then this page checks each one for warning signs and compares its price with what the card sold for.

          Does a low score mean the listing is safe?

          No. It only means you found fewer warning signs in the answers you gave. Scammers can fake reviews, photos and friendly messages, so always test the card and pay in a way that protects you.

          How do I find the typical sold price?

          Open the eBay sold listings link, look at cards that really sold instead of asking prices, and take the middle price of the last ten or so. Skip boxes, coolers and parts.

          Why does the way I pay count for so much?

          Money sent by wire, crypto, gift card or a payment app to a stranger is very hard to get back. Card and checkout payments usually give you a way to dispute the charge, which is why they add few or no points.

          Can it tell me if a card is fake?

          Not from a listing alone. It points you to the checks that can: the ports and connectors, the details GPU-Z shows and a stress test. See the test guide above.

          Is anything I type sent anywhere?

          No. It all stays in your browser on this device, including the listings you save.
